How IPvX works

Four parts: the registry, the packet layer, names and route choice. Below: what of them is already implemented and tested, then the historical prototype on which delivery without IP was measured.

Registry: an address from the blockchain

In the original papers an IPvX address has three parts: the interconnection side, the entity (NFT) and the connection rule (smart contract). It is not handed out by the ICANN → RIR → provider chain: ownership and rules are recorded in a registry anyone can verify.

In BCP 0.2 these are three contracts. NodeRegistry binds a node's NFT to its owner and keys. TransitPolicy and ConnectionPolicy define whom traffic may pass through and whom a node may connect to; a rule takes effect only when both sides agree. A BCP packet carries the recipient's owner, NFT and connection rule.

The REG-001 experiment tested these contracts on the Sepolia test chain: a different owner cannot change a node, and a change takes effect for nodes after 14–19 minutes, once the block is finalized. REG-001 results →

BCP 0.2

BCP 0.2 is the IPvX packet layer and registry. The registry contracts were tested on the live Sepolia chain: REG-001 ran in read mode Q, and R1 was closed on 2026-11-06 by the REG-001-LC light-client read on its replacement criterion, the current state; the S1–S3 values remain mode Q observations. Packet delivery driven by the registry (NET-001) has not run.

Status snapshot: 2026-11-06

Implemented in code

Packet format, NodeRegistry, TransitPolicy, ConnectionPolicy, the EL/CL adapter and core integration. Contract reads are pinned to one finalized blockHash.

Checked offline

77 runner tests and 14 plan checks. Temporary keys, an in-memory EVM, synthetic headers and memory ports; no network or chain-finality measurement.

Next network experiment

NET-001: the protocol is accepted in two variants, on test state and on live registry state; neither has run. The live variant reads the registry through the Helios light client: mode LC replaced mode V on 2026-11-05, so no own Sepolia node is needed. Helios is not running on the stand: the temporary client used for the read was removed. The separate NET-001 stand VM is not allocated, and the snapshot refresh rule in NET-001-L is an open condition. Launch admission is closed.

Names: the IRS resolver

In the second original paper names come from the registry rather than the DNS hierarchy. The IRS resolver answers ordinary name queries but takes its data from the blockchain. Names are flat and do not depend on the ICANN root zone; the paper shows a working zone iedn.ipvx.

This track has not started in the research yet. The NAME-001 protocol will be written after the NET-001 experiment, when the registry already gives nodes real state.

Route choice

Contracts between NFTs define the permitted paths but do not pick the best one among them. The choice can be made by price, latency or contract terms; the original paper assigns this task to AI. Route choice under such contracts has not yet been tested in the research.

Where IP remains

IPvX replaces IP addressing and DNS rather than building on top of them. During the transition IPvX packets can be carried over existing IP; IP is then only a carrier, not part of the address.

For now IP remains where it cannot yet be avoided: this site, management of the stand machines, model installation and reading the registry from an Ethereum node. In the delivery experiments frames between nodes travel without IP. Connecting sites through an IP tunnel would be the transitional mode, and it would not prove that IP is gone from the underlying network.

Ethernet frames · EtherType 0x88B5

External management, virtual machine startup and this website use IP. The shared physical network also carries management IP traffic. Native delivery within the guest systems uses neither IP nor DNS.

  1. A prepares the task

    Model A prepares a response. The IPvX service addresses the message to B’s ID, signs and encrypts it.

  2. Request travels through R1

    A sends a frame to neighbor R1; R1 selects the next hop to B. A receipt confirms admission, not correctness.

  3. B reviews and replies

    Model B receives the task and produces a review. The reply travels as a separate message through R1 back to A.

  4. A produces the final answer

    Model A uses the review for its final answer. The decision field matched the reference in two of four tasks.

What was verified

4 tasks and 48 signed deliveries in native mode: 40 echo checks and 8 task messages. The IP control used the same application hops.

Per-hop timing, queue wait and the reason for route selection are absent from the public record. The animation does not measure them.

External networks, open admission, large-scale routing and bare-metal physical servers without IP remain separate untested goals.

Historical prototype layers

  1. Identity

    The node creates its own keys. Its ID is derived from the public key and persists when its network location changes. Trust and admission are configured separately.

  2. Task

    Agents exchange structured tasks, evidence and results. They do not need website pages for this. Compatible task formats are still necessary.

  3. Routing

    A node receives signed announcements from neighbors and selects the next hop from its local table. The central observer does not relay tasks or assign routes.

  4. Delivery

    In the native experiment, the packet becomes Ethernet frames with EtherType 0x88B5. The MAC identifies a neighbor on the local link; the ID identifies the final agent.

From prototype to BCP

In the historical prototype the ID is derived from a key. In BCP, NodeRegistry binds an NFT identifier to the owner and keys, while TransitPolicy and ConnectionPolicy define permitted paths. These are different admission models; their results do not substitute for each other. BCP 0.2 has no names yet: their place will be taken by the IRS resolver.

Node without TCP/IP: verified experiment

In the completed experiment, local models ran in entire guest operating systems without INET or IPv6 on separate physical hosts. Ethernet frames were observed inside guests; external-machine management retained IP. The actual hypervisor underlay has not been separately verified from its configuration.
