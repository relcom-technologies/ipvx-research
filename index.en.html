IPvX — addresses, names and routes instead of IP and DNS

The source of truth is a blockchain registry. A node is an NFT, a name is a registry record, a path is a contract between NFTs in a smart contract. IPvX packets travel directly over the transmission medium; during the transition they can be carried over existing IP.

Where we are now

What has been tested

The registry works on the live Sepolia test chain: node registration, owner rights and two-sided consent to a path. A registry change takes effect after 14–19 minutes, not instantly.

Separately, delivery of Ethernet frames without IP and DNS was measured between five machines — but among pre-admitted nodes, without the blockchain registry.

Joining the registry and delivery is the next step. Names and route choice have not been tested yet. This portal itself runs over ordinary IP, DNS and HTTPS.

The four parts of IPvX

  1. A registry instead of address allocation

    A node gets its identity as an NFT in a blockchain registry. Owner, keys and path rules are recorded in smart contracts instead of being handed out by the ICANN → RIR → provider chain.

    Tested on Sepolia

    REG-001 results →

  2. Packets without IP

    A packet carries the recipient's owner, NFT and connection rule and travels directly in Ethernet frames. The MAC names the nearest neighbor; the registry address names the final node.

    Measured without the registry

    Five-machine experiment →Live stand ↗

  3. Names without the DNS hierarchy

    The IRS resolver answers name queries from registry records. Names are flat and do not depend on the ICANN root zone.

    Not started

    How it is designed →

  4. Route choice

    Contracts between NFTs define the permitted paths. Among them a route is chosen by price, latency or contract terms; the original paper assigns this task to AI.

    Not started

    How it is designed →
